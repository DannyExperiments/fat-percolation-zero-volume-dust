# Q1294 primary-source comparison

27 November 2026. The catalog points to Broman, van de Brug, Camia, Joosten and Meester, *Fat fractal percolation and k-fractal percolation*, ALEA 9(2) (2012), 279–301, Open Problem 1.10, printed p. 283, following dichotomy (1.2)–(1.3) in Theorem 1.8. In the independent N-adic model with nondecreasing retention probabilities and positive product, the question asks whether the singleton components can carry all the positive Lebesgue measure on nonextinction. Theorem 1.7 gives positive measure on nonextinction. Theorem 1.9(ii) uses the additional condition that the product of p_n to powers N^(dn) is positive. Open Problem 1.10 and the antecedent thesis remark abbreviate that exponent to N^n in referring to the theorem. The proof in §4.3 counts N^(dn) level-n cubes. For p_n=1−1/(n+1)^2 the ordinary product is 1/2 while either weighted product vanishes. Theorem 1.11 gives equivalent criteria for the positive-volume connected part in dimension two. Its premises still require a point-connectivity estimate. [Final ALEA article](https://alea.math.cnrs.fr/articles/v9/09-12.pdf#page=4) · [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.6834) · [Van de Brug’s dissertation, Remark 6.8](https://research.vu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/42205157/complete%20dissertation.pdf).

Kolossváry and Troscheit retain the exact problem as Question 7.3 in the publicly accessible version of *Recent Progress on Fractal Percolation*, after reviewing the volume dichotomy in §2. Troscheit’s site lists the Birkhäuser proceedings contribution as accepted. This comparison uses the available arXiv version. [Survey §§2 and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08150v1#S7) · [Troscheit’s publications](https://www.troscheit.eu/research/).

The closest construction is Anttila, Eriksson-Bique and Pyörälä, *Quasisymmetric mappings on two variants of fractal percolation*, arXiv:2510.06900. Section 3.1 uses exactly one missing subcube at every node of a fixed N^(md)-child block tree. Theorem 4.1 already iterates a quadratic child-failure bound backwards under a uniform thickness hypothesis. Theorem 5.1 uses it for the same inhomogeneous N-adic model with p_n→1 to obtain existence of these subtrees with probability bounded away from zero inside any sufficiently deep retained cube. Theorem 2.1 proves full conformal Hausdorff dimension. With exactly one child deleted at each split, the subset has Lebesgue volume zero, computed from the fraction (1−N^(−md)) retained at each split. Positive probability of finding such subsets does not give a volume estimate for their union. The present proof uses every good descendant, retaining the actual summable local defect probabilities in the recurrence instead of bounding them all by one constant. It then proves that almost every surviving point is eventually in an unbroken chain of good cubes. [Section 3.1 and Theorems 4.1 and 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06900v1).

Falconer and Grimmett use recursive good cubes and compatible neighboring refinements for crossings in homogeneous fractal percolation. Their proof around printed pp. 482–484 explains the shared-face issue. The new argument uses a two-step fixed grid, deleting at most one child, and preserves an aligned contact through every finite depth. Buczolich, Järvenpää, Järvenpää, Keleti and Pöyhtäri also use probabilistically abundant hereditarily good cubes in homogeneous fractal percolation to control rectifiability. Both methods predate this proof. Neither source supplies almost-everywhere connected coverage in the inhomogeneous positive-product model. [Falconer–Grimmett, 1992](https://www.statslab.cam.ac.uk/~grg1000/papers/jtp5-465.pdf#page=18) · [Buczolich et al., 2021, §5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2021.107906).

Bounded verdict. Source match and prior method overlap established with high confidence. Searches on the numbered question, its survey restatement, zero-volume dust and nearby results produced no earlier exact resolution under the ordinary product assumption. Confidence is moderate for this limited priority finding. The proposed theorem addresses singleton-component Lebesgue measure in d≥2 and allows nonmonotonic probabilities. It leaves the number and organization of the connected components undetermined.
